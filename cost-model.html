Cost and planning

What a Build Costs

The software is free. The cost is people’s time, and most of that is the judgment of someone who knows the market. This page says what the cost is made of, what moves it, and how far the figures can be trusted.

The short answer

  • Nothing to license. The engines and the guides are open. There is no fee to DeeperPoint for using them.
  • A few hundred hours of skilled work. For a market of moderate complexity, a new knowledge base and a first demonstration come to roughly 215 to 355 hours.
  • Very little cash. AI fees and tools for that build run from about US$100 to US$1,200. The demonstration needs no server.
  • The costly part cannot be handed off. A developer can stand up the software knowing nothing about your industry. Only someone who knows the market can say where its deals fail and why.

These are costs to whoever does the build. They are not prices, and they come from a small number of builds. The last section says how small. Cash amounts on this page are in US dollars, because that is how the AI services bill.

What is being built

A thin market is one where willing buyers and sellers exist and the deal still does not happen. DeeperPoint’s toolkit builds a marketplace for such a market in three stages, and a build can stop after any of them.

  1. The wiki. What the market knows, written as pages tied to their sources: what a certificate means, which requirement has a lawful alternative, how the two sides describe the same thing in different words. An AI model drafts; a person who knows the market rules on what stands.
  2. The twin. The marketplace software configured for the market and filled with invented, clearly marked participants, so a sponsor can watch the market behave before any real person joins.
  3. The marketplace. The twin with real participants and a sponsor who runs it.

Beside these sits an optional question page, called Ask, where people put questions to the wiki and get answers with citations. See the whole path →

People’s time is nearly all of it. Cash is a few percent. Sponsors often expect the screens to be the expensive part and the knowledge to be a matter of uploading documents. It is the other way round: the screens already exist, and the knowledge is yours alone.

What costs nothing

  • The software. The three engines and the wiki engine are open source.
  • The method. The guides and the build recipe are free to use and adapt.
  • The screens. The twin’s seven screens come with the framework and are configured, not written.
  • Hosting a first demonstration. A first twin is a folder of files that opens from a disk. No server, no database, no account.

What each visit costs

A twin can run in three modes. They differ in what a visitor can do, and in what each visit costs in AI fees.

ModeWhat the visitor getsAI fees per session (US$)Hosting
ShowcaseMatches and deal briefs worked out in advance. The same results every time; nothing can be changed$0None. Static files, on any free web host or a disk
LiveMatching and deal briefs produced as the visitor explores. Every visit is different; nothing is kept afterwards$0.10 to $0.17One modest server, no graphics processor
FullEverything working, with histories kept between sessions. A guided presentation, not self-service$0.50 to $1.00The same server, plus analytics

Showcase costs nothing per visit because the AI work was done once, when the twin was built, for US$5 to US$50 in total. A thousand sessions therefore cost nothing in Showcase, US$100 to US$170 in Live, and US$500 to US$1,000 in Full.

The per-session figures are design estimates, not yet measured. In Live and Full a visitor can supply their own AI account, and then the fees are theirs. A marketplace with real participants has not been run yet, so its cost per session is not known.

What moves the cost

  1. Whether your industry already has a wiki. Knowledge that is true across an industry is built once and read by every market in it. If it exists, the first 55 to 110 hours are already spent by someone else.
  2. How complex the market is. Each kind of participant, each jurisdiction and each negotiable term adds vocabulary to define and requirements to research.
  3. How many hard requirements there are. The method works through the market’s requirements one by one and records the ways through each. More requirements, more research. This is also what makes the work finite.
  4. The state of the sources. Public documents are cheap to work from. Paywalled standards can only be cited. Knowledge nobody wrote down has to come from the expert directly.
  5. How quickly the expert can answer. Every stage stops for a decision. This does not change the hours much, and it changes the calendar more than anything else.
  6. How much review the material needs. Sensitive content, outside reviewers and community rules about data each add a step.
  7. Whether you design your own screens. The provided screens take days to configure. A custom design adds 80 to 200 hours.
  8. Which mode the twin runs in. Showcase needs no hosting and costs nothing per visit. Live and Full need a server and pay AI fees on every session, as the table above shows.

Some things do not move it: the number of people who view a demonstration, the choice of AI model, and software licences.

Two points in this order are fixed. The wiki is reviewed and closed before anything is built on it. And the schema is frozen before the participants are generated, because they are generated once: a renamed field is free before that point and costly after it.

What these figures leave out

  • Launching a real marketplace. Recruiting participants, legal review, support and operations belong to the sponsor and run for months. They depend on the sponsor’s standing in the industry, not on anything measured here.
  • Keeping the wiki current. It is the sponsor’s standing job once the market runs. No figure exists yet.
  • A live instance. The hosting requirement is known. The hours to stand one up are not yet measured.
  • Reviewers’ and testers’ time.
  • Changes to the engines. The figures assume the market can be handled by configuration.

How good these numbers are

Not very, yet, and it is better to say so. One wiki has been built by this method and two twins. No sponsored marketplace exists. Nobody logged hours on any of them.

  • The wiki figure is inferred from the working record of one wiki: about 45 batches of research over five weeks, producing some 450 shared pages.
  • The twin figure is the written plan for the second twin, step by step. That twin is still being built.
  • The multipliers for simple and complex markets are estimates. Both twins so far are of moderate complexity.
  • An earlier estimate expected a second build to cost about two-thirds of the first. The second twin’s plan came out higher instead, for reasons it names. The saving from experience has not been seen yet.
