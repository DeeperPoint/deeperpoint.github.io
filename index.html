Markets don't make themselves.

Thousands of markets never form: willing buyers and sellers who cannot find each other, trust each other, or close a deal. We call them thin markets. Eighty years of economics explains why they stay that way, and the frictions that keep them thin can now be engineered away.

Why Markets Don’t Form

Why don’t some markets form?

Buyers and sellers who cannot find, trust or deal with each other. The frictions are known, documented over eighty years, and they repeat across thousands of markets.

What We’re Building

What are you building?

A free, open-source market engine, built once and dressed for each market: semantic matching, confidential disclosure, and a Deal Brief that hands off to the parties’ own professionals. Fifteen cards show what it does about each resistance.

Built in the Open

The frictions that keep markets thin are common across thousands of markets, so the layer that fights them can be built once and reused. DeeperPoint is building that layer as free, MIT-licensed, open-source software: adopt it, fork it, build on it, and nobody can raise the price or lock you in. There is no license fee, no usage tier, and no upsell behind the demo. Getting a new market engine adopted is a thin-market problem too, and price, procurement and lock-in are its biggest frictions.

The project is self-funded, with no investors and no board, and it is open to anyone who wants to take part: with code, with knowledge of a market, with a market to test on, or with a hard question.
