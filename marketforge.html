MarketForge

The map and the method for building a marketplace in a thin market

MarketForge is not another engine. It is the public design map of the toolkit: what the parts are, the order in which they are used, how far each has got, and where to read the detail. It is written for two readers: a sponsor who knows a market, and a developer who will stand one up.

Open MarketForge on GitHub → Where to start

The path

A market is built in one direction, and it starts with what the market knows. Each later stage sends what it could not answer back to the first. Asking the wiki is an optional service beside the path, not a step on it.

1. The wiki Knowledge

The engine is generic; the knowledge is not. What a certificate means in one trade, which requirement has a lawful alternative, how the two sides describe the same thing in different words: only people in the market know these. That knowledge is kept as a wiki of plain-text pages, each tied to its sources, drafted with an AI model and ruled on by a person.

Only the sponsor can supply it. It comes first, and it is never finished. The knowledge a market runs on →

2. The twin Simulation

The marketplace engine configured for the market and filled with synthetic, marked participants, so a sponsor can watch the market behave before any real person joins. Casual interest is satisfied by Ask; actual matching is demonstrated in the twin.

Back to the wiki: values and requirements that no page backs.

3. The marketplace Real participants

The twin with real participants and a sponsor. Synthetic profiles never enter it. The wiki stays, and keeping it current is the sponsor's standing job.

Back to the wiki: blocked matches and unanswered questions.

Optional · beside the path

Ask

A question page over the wiki lets a sponsor, a tester or anyone who needs to be informed ask it. The answers come only from the wiki, with citations, and the questions it could not answer go back to the wiki as the next research.

It is valuable, and it is not a step in the build: a twin and a marketplace can be built without it. It can be offered for a time, as an early check on the wiki, or kept for good as a service in its own right: a broker answering for clients, a software marketplace helping its users, a person finding their own way, or any setting whose purpose is to educate. The same service can also be offered inside the twin and the marketplace.

Tracked at every stage

More than one way through

Most tasks a participant must get through have more than one way through them, and the people a thin market fails are often the ones who know only the obvious way. A requirement written as "certificate required" can silently refuse people who could lawfully be hired another way.

So the wiki records, for each task, the ways through it: who grants each, what it confers, what it still requires. It says a path exists. It never says a person qualifies, and it never invents a path.

The toolkit, part by part

The first three diagnose. The wiki engine and the question page hold a market's knowledge and let people ask it. CommonContext, ClientSynth and Cosolvent build the twin and the marketplace on that knowledge. MarketForge is the order in which it is all done.

The twin

Three twins are being built, in more than one configuration: scripted, computed once in advance, and run live by an AI model. They cover a manufacturing capacity exchange and a job-matching market. The schemas were written by hand; the wiki backs and checks them.
