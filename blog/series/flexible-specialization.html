The Siren Song of Flexible Specialization

From the artisan workshops of northern Italy to Magna's purposefully small automotive plants to Ontario's aerospace manufacturing corridor — this five-part series traces the history, logic, and future of flexible specialization: the model in which fragmented specialist firms, properly coordinated, consistently outcompete large vertically-integrated manufacturers on quality and responsiveness.

    Workshop Notes: The Italian Impannatore — What a Weaving Cluster Can Teach the World
    How Italian textile clusters became the world's finest manufacturing ecosystem — and what that model suggests about what could be built, with better tools, in other places.
    March 24, 2026 · 8 min read
  • 2
    Workshop Notes: Flexible Specialization Is Not Italian — It's Universal
    The Italian textile cluster model is not an anomaly — it appears across industries and continents. Examining six other cases reveals what makes these systems thrive, what makes them fail, and where AI-mediated coordination could matter most.
    March 24, 2026 · 9 min read

    Workshop Notes: Magna's Small-Plant Model — An Industrial District Built on Purpose
    Magna International grew into one of the world's largest Tier-1 automotive suppliers by deliberately doing the opposite of what conventional industrial wisdom prescribed — keeping plants small, specialized, and entrepreneurially autonomous. The model is a Canadian proof-of-concept for the flexible specialization thesis.
    March 24, 2026 · 8 min read
  • 4
    Workshop Notes: Why Traditional Flexible Specialization Fails Under Pressure
    The historical attempts at independent flexible specialization networks successfully mastered craft, but failed at scale. Analyzing the friction of human coordination reveals exactly what breaks under global competition.
    March 24, 2026 · 6 min read

    Workshop Notes: The Cosolvent Cooperation Marketplace
