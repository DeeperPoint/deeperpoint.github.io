The Thin Market Problem

Canada's Primary Reserve Force numbers approximately 30,000 Class A, B, and C reservists who balance civilian employment with part-time military service. The Reserve Force's operational and training demands — summer tasking, domestic operations, NATO deployments — require civilian employers to grant military leave, sometimes on short notice, sometimes for extended periods. This creates a systemic employment friction that the current system manages through legal protection (the National Defence Act prohibits employment termination for Reserve service) and advocacy (the Canadian Defence and Security Industries Reserve Friendly Employer program), but does not resolve through matching: reservists finding employers who genuinely benefit from what Reserve service develops, and employers finding reservists whose military skills fill a specific workforce capability gap. The matching problem runs in both directions. A Reserve signals regiment warrant officer who is also a civilian IT network architect brings back from each training period applied experience in austere communications, physical security of network infrastructure, and leadership under degraded conditions — all of which are directly applicable to critical infrastructure protection work that his civilian employer claims to prioritize. An infantry company commander who is a project manager in civilian life returns from a six-month NATO enhanced Forward Presence deployment with a depth of ambiguous, high-stakes decision-making experience that his employer has never identified as a talent asset. The Reserve Force produces these capabilities systematically. The civilian labour market almost never captures them because no matching infrastructure connects reserve skills to employer needs at the level of specificity required.

Dominant Forces
  • Employer military literacy gap — most civilian HR departments treat reserve service as a scheduling complication rather than a talent development multiplier; they do not know what skills reservists build during taskings and deployments
  • Reservist self-presentation failure — reservists, like releasing regular force members, are trained to understate operational experience; they do not present their military competencies in language that civilian performance management systems recognize
  • Military leave risk perception — employers who have never managed a military leave event overestimate its operational disruption; employers who recruit deliberately from the reservist population have built scheduling and coverage systems that reduce the disruption to near-zero
  • Skills returnability — reservists return from extended taskings with updated skills in leadership, logistics, communications, and operational medicine that employers who had planned intentionally for the absence can capture; employers who granted leave reluctantly have not planned for reintegration
  • Geographic clustering — reserve units are distributed across Canada including in communities where reservist employer options are limited; remote and rural employers who have defence-relevant work (resource extraction, critical infrastructure, emergency services) are underrepresented in reserve employer recruitment
How DeeperPoint Helps

MarketForge lists reservists by unit type, trade, rank, and the civil-military skills crosswalk that their specific reserve service builds. Employers post positions with either an explicit reserve-friendly status or a specific military skill need (leadership development, emergency management, communications security, logistics coordination). The match surfaces reservist candidates to employers based on civil-military skill fit and employer reserve-readiness profile. Knowledge Slot provides the military leave legal framework, reserve employer best practice guides, and the CEOTP (Cadet Organizations Employment and Training Plan) and other reserve support program details relevant to employers.

Economic Upside

30,000 reservists represent a systematically undercapitalized workforce development asset for Canadian employers. A platform that generates 300 intentional reservist placements per year with employers who have been matched on reserve skill value — at a $3,000 employer facilitation fee — produces $900,000 in annual revenue. The Canadian Armed Forces' Strategic Reserve Reserve Force Strategy and Employment Equity programs provide grant funding for reserve employer development initiatives; DND's employer support programs (currently operating through informal advocacy) would benefit from a structured matching platform to document and scale their impact.

Narrative Story
The Mass Casualty Experience Nobody Asked About

Characters: Corporal Anita — Registered Nurse and Class A reservist with 32 Canadian Brigade Group Field Ambulance in Hamilton; her hospital grants military leave but has never asked what she does during it, David — Chief Nursing Officer at the same hospital; has been trying to build mass-casualty response capability in the ED for two years; has sent three nurses to external courses; hasn't asked Anita, The weekend — Anita just returned from a three-day mass-casualty exercise where she ran triage for a simulated 200-casualty event; David doesn't know this happened

Act One: The Course That Wasn't Enough

David had sent his third nurse to the mass-casualty management course in September. The course was good — a three-day program at a regional emergency management centre, START triage, incident command integration, surge capacity protocols. Each nurse who went came back certified and better prepared than before. The problem was that the course gave them the framework and a tabletop exercise and two days of scenario role-play. What it could not give them was the muscle memory of actually running triage for 200 casualties under simulated operational pressure, with real medical colleagues making real-time calls, with the confusion and communication breakdowns and equipment failures that field exercises produce.

He had been told by a colleague at a Toronto hospital that his best mass-casualty triage nurse had spent eight years in the army reserve. He had started wondering, quietly, whether any of his nurses had similar backgrounds.

He had not asked.

Act Two: The Three-Day Exercise

Anita had returned from the exercise the previous Sunday. It had been the largest field ambulance exercise the brigade had run in four years — four units, 200 simulated casualties with moulage, an austere medical post with limited supplies, and a communications architecture that had partially failed on day two in exactly the way real communications fail. She had run the triage post for fourteen hours on day one. She had cross-trained two nursing officers from another unit on a resuscitation protocol that the field ambulance had adapted from a recent NATO medical bulletin.

She had submitted her military leave request for the weekend in August. The ward manager had approved it without comment. When she returned, nobody had asked where she had been.

Act Three: The Match

When David posted the hospital's ED position on the platform — a senior staff nurse with interest in emergency preparedness and mass-casualty response — he had included "military or emergency services experience considered an asset" because the colleague's story had stayed with him.

The platform matched Anita's reservist profile to his posting. Her competence description, in the military-to-civilian crosswalk the platform had built from her trade and rank: "Mass-casualty triage (START/SALT), incident command integration, austere-environment clinical decision-making, field resuscitation protocols, 200+ casualty exercise command experience."

David called her the same week. The conversation lasted forty minutes. He offered her a lead role in the ED's emergency preparedness committee. She accepted.

He also asked if she would be willing to run an internal mass-casualty simulation for the ED team using the field exercise structure her unit had developed. She said yes.

It cost the hospital nothing. It was better than the course.

Characters are fictional. The Canadian Primary Reserve Force, 32 Canadian Brigade Group Field Ambulance, National Defence Act military leave protections, and mass-casualty triage frameworks are real. DeeperPoint is building the matching infrastructure this market requires.

Government Partnership
Canadian Reserve Friendly Employer Matching Platform

DND's Chief of Reserves and Employer Support (CRES) currently manages reserve employer relationships through advocates and volunteers; a platform that systemizes the matching and provides employers with structured value proposition replaces an advocacy model with an operational platform DND would co-fund to scale its reserve force sustainability.

💵 Annual employer certification subscription (Reserve Friendly Employer designation with verified matching); per-placement facilitation fee; DND employer support program grant; provincial critical infrastructure operator partnership
Insurance Product
Reserve Employer HR Support Subscription and Military Leave Insurance

Employers matched with reservists have an ongoing HR management need: military leave policy compliance, operational continuity planning for extended taskings, and insurance coverage for the revenue disruption that a key employee's extended absence creates. The platform has the employer's business profile, the reservist's tasking schedule, and the military leave legal framework. Extending into an HR support subscription and a military leave business interruption insurance product creates recurring professional services and insurance revenue from the same employers the matching platform acquired.

💵 Annual HR support subscription per reserve-friendly employer (reserve employment law guidance, military leave policy templates, operational continuity planning tools; $1,500-5,000/year); military leave business interruption insurance facilitation fee covering the cost of temporary replacement workers during extended taskings (annual premium 0.5-1% of covered employee salary); reserve employer certification management; platform earns HR services and insurance facilitation revenue from every employer it matches with a reservist
