The Thin Market Problem

National and regional retail tenants evaluating new locations compare leasing packages from multiple potential sites before committing to a market. A well-prepared leasing package—professional interior and exterior photography, drone aerial views of the site and trade area, a digital virtual tour, a demographic analysis showing trade area population and income profile, daily traffic counts with pedestrian and vehicular breakdowns, and co-tenancy comparisons—can be the difference between a national retailer's site committee putting a location on their shortlist or passing it over. Independent retail plaza owners and smaller regional REITs often present basic packages—a PDF floor plan, a few iPhone photographs, and a broker's broker opinion of market rent—competing against REIT-managed properties whose investor relations teams produce professional-grade leasing materials as a matter of course. The gap is not the quality of the property; it is the quality of the package presentation. Assembling a competitive leasing package—hiring a commercial real estate photographer, a drone operator, a virtual tour producer, a GIS demographic data analyst, and a traffic study firm—requires coordinating four to five specialty vendors that most independent landlords have never contracted before and whose market is not organized to find.

Dominant Forces
  • Offering complexity — a competitive national-tenant leasing package requires professional photography, drone footage, virtual tour production, GIS demographic analysis, and traffic count data—each from distinct specialty service providers
  • Participant scarcity — commercial real estate drone operators certified for flight in suburban trade areas (NAV CANADA RPAS certification, building envelope experience) are distinct from residential real estate drone photographers
  • Data source specificity — trade area demographic analysis using Environics Analytics, Statistics Canada DMTI data, or Esri Business Analyst requires GIS analysts familiar with commercial real estate use applications
  • Timeline coherence — all marketing assets must reflect the same seasonal state of the property and be produced within the same site visit window for visual consistency
  • National tenant threshold — national retailers' real estate teams have seen thousands of leasing packages; a package that falls below a quality threshold is dismissed without site visit
How DeeperPoint Helps

Semantic matching encodes service provider profiles (specialization, commercial real estate experience vs. residential, geographic coverage, certification standing for drone operations, GIS data platform access, production turnaround timeline) against landlord opportunity signals (property type, location, tenant mix, specific package components needed, budget, national tenant target category). Package coordination—scheduling all site visit services on the same day to minimize disruption and ensure visual consistency—is a matchable capability, not just a service list.

Economic Upside

A 15,000 sq.ft. junior anchor space leasing at $22/sq.ft. net generates $330,000/year in base rent with typical 10-year terms. A professional leasing package that earns a national tenant site visit where a poor package would not—and a site visit that converts to a lease negotiation—is worth the entire leasing package cost in the first month of stabilized occupancy. The marketing package investment ($8,000–$18,000 for a complete professional package) pays back in under two weeks of occupied rent.

Narrative Story
The Junior Anchor Gap

Characters: Frank — independent owner, 68,000 sq.ft. anchored retail plaza, Barrie, Ontario; 8,200 sq.ft. junior anchor vacancy, Melissa — commercial real estate photographer and leasing package producer, Barrie/Simcoe County

Act A — The National Tenant Problem

Frank's plaza is well-located and well-occupied: 68,000 sq.ft., a grocery anchor, a bank, four food service tenants, and a national pharmacy. The 8,200 sq.ft. junior anchor space at the north end has been vacant for 22 months since a sporting goods tenant vacated. He has paid a commercial broker to represent the space.

His broker has sent the listing to three national junior anchor prospects—a discount fashion retailer, a dollar-format retailer, and a pet supply chain. All three are actively expanding in Southern Ontario. None have responded to follow-up contacts from his broker.

The broker explained it plainly: the national tenants' real estate teams review forty to sixty opportunity packages per week. Frank's package—a PDF floor plan, an excerpt from the appraisal with trade area demographics, and three photographs Frank had taken with his phone—did not earn enough attention to trigger a site visit request.

Act B — The Story

Frank asked his broker what a competitive package looked like. The broker described what large REIT properties submit: professional photography, aerial drone of the site and the surrounding trade area showing competitive retail density, a digital virtual tour, an Environics Analytics demographic report for 1, 3, and 5 kilometre rings, and a traffic count summary.

Frank had never assembled any of this. He asked his broker to help him find the vendors. His broker's referrals covered commercial photography—one person he knew—but not drone operations (the photographer didn't do drone), virtual tours (neither the broker nor the photographer had a recommendation), or demographic analysis (his broker used a service his firm subscribed to but Frank couldn't access).

He spent three weeks finding and coordinating vendors. The photographer and drone operator he found separately couldn't coordinate their visit dates until three weeks out. The demographic analysis vendor he found through Google could produce the report but not in the Environics Analytics format that matched the national tenant template his broker had described.

Melissa produces commercial real estate leasing packages for independent landlords across Barrie and Simcoe County. She has a standing relationship with an NAV CANADA-certified drone operator who shoots commercial sites. She uses a GIS analyst who has Environics Analytics access and produces trade area reports in national retailer-compatible format. She has a virtual tour production partner. She can coordinate a same-week site visit across all three components and produce a complete package in 12 business days.

Frank found her profile on the MarketForge leasing package assembly platform after his broker mentioned it to him. He had lost six months of broker outreach to an inadequate package before finding her.

She produced the package. It went to the same three national retailers. The pet supply chain requested a site visit within three weeks of receiving the package. They signed a 10-year lease five months later.

Act C — Why This Market Stays Broken Without Infrastructure

Melissa's coordinated leasing package service existed. She had done this for nine other independent landlords in Simcoe County. She was findable on the MarketForge platform. She was not findable through the broker referral and Google search path Frank had been using for three weeks.

The barrier between Frank's 22-month vacancy and the pet supply chain's 10-year lease was a marketing package quality gap that a 12-business-day production cycle could have addressed—if Frank had found Melissa in month one rather than month twenty-two.

Thin market infrastructure makes coordinated commercial leasing package producers discoverable at the moment a landlord's broker explains why the existing package isn't working—not after twenty-two months of vacancy.

Characters are fictional. National retailer real estate team package standards, Environics Analytics trade area demographic platforms, and Barrie Ontario's retail market are real. DeeperPoint is building the infrastructure this story describes.

Logistics Extension
Retail Tenant Consolidated Logistics and Delivery Coordination

Retail plazas that have been tenanted through the platform's leasing matching service have an ongoing operational coordination problem: multiple tenants receiving deliveries on overlapping schedules, competing for loading dock access, and paying retail freight rates on small individual orders. The platform has the plaza profile, the tenant mix, and the lease terms. Extending into a shared logistics coordination service creates a recurring plaza operations revenue stream from the same landlord and tenant relationships the leasing matching business established.
