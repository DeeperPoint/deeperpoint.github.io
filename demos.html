2 · What the market knows skills-based job matching

A market is built on what it knows. Here are three services over one body of knowledge: the wiki itself, a question page over it, and a guide written from it. The market is skills-based job matching, worked through one specific case: Indigenous workers and the employers who want to hire them.

Why this example. Matching what a worker can do to what a job needs is a general problem. Adding the Indigenous dimension makes it specific, and a specific case is more useful for diagnosis and for learning than a general one. The idea came from casual conversations with people who know the field. Nobody commissioned it: it is DeeperPoint’s own construction. Everything in these demos is drawn solely from public sources, and none of it speaks for any Indigenous person, community or organization.

By request

The wiki

A wiki on workplace cultures and what stands between Indigenous job seekers and employers. Its pages are drafted with an AI model from public sources and ruled on by a person.

What is real
A live wiki of sourced pages.
What to keep in mind
It is one wiki so far, and it is never finished: what the later stages cannot answer comes back to it.
Try this
Open a page on something you know well, and check its sources.

Ask

A question page over the wiki. The answers come only from the wiki’s shared pages, with citations, and it says so when the wiki cannot answer.

The Two paths guide

A support worker describes one worker and one job, and gets a printable guide that sets two ways of handling the start side by side: on your own, and with the supports the wiki lists.

What is real
Each guide is written live by an AI model from the wiki’s pages, and every item cites one.
What to keep in mind

3 · Watch a market run three twins, from staged to live

A twin is the marketplace engine filled with synthetic participants, so a market can be watched before any real person joins. These three go from nothing computed, to computed once, to computed live. All three are still being built. As they, and possibly others, become viewable they will be added to this page.

Open to everyone

A guided tour of a three-sided marketplace: a machine shop, a buyer and an inspection lab, from anonymous match to revealed identity to Deal Brief.

The workflow and the screens, step for step.
What is staged
Everything you see was written in advance. No matching runs and no AI model is consulted.

The job-matching twin

Skills-based job matching

The marketplace engine configured for the job-matching example in section 2 and filled with synthetic job seekers and employers. It is built on that section’s wiki, and the note on why this example applies here too.

What is real
The engine’s matching, computed live over the population. The schema was written by hand; the wiki backs and checks it.
What is staged
Every participant is synthetic and marked as such. The current population is a stand-in, generated by an AI model, until the one from ClientSynth replaces it.
