2 · One market, along the path Indigenous job matching

A market is built in one direction: first a wiki of what the market knows, then a twin filled with synthetic participants. Here are both stages for the same market, so you can see what the second takes from the first.

By invitation

The wiki, and Ask

A wiki on workplace cultures and what stands between Indigenous job seekers and employers, with a question page over it. The answers come only from the wiki’s shared pages, with citations, and it says so when the wiki cannot answer.

What is real
A live wiki of sourced pages, and live answers drawn from it.
What to keep in mind
It is built over one wiki so far. That testers’ questions improve the wiki is the design; the evidence is not in yet.
Try this
Ask something you know the answer to, then follow the citations and see whether they hold.
How Ask works → Ask for access →
By invitation

The job-matching twin

The marketplace engine configured for this market and filled with synthetic job seekers and employers, so the market can be watched before any real person joins.

What is real
The engine’s matching over the population. The schema was written by hand; the wiki backs and checks it.
What is staged
Every participant is synthetic and marked as such. The current population is a stand-in, generated by an AI model, until the one from ClientSynth replaces it.
Try this
Put a question to Ask first, then look for the same requirement in a match.

What is real
The workflow and the screens, step for step.
What is staged
Everything you see was written in advance. No matching runs and no AI model is consulted.

Twin on precomputed matches

The capacity-exchange twin: a synthetic population, matched by the real matching logic, with the results computed once and frozen.

What is real
The matches, scores, Deal Briefs and stories are the engine’s own output over the population.
What is staged
Nothing is computed while you watch, so the same choices give the same results every time.
